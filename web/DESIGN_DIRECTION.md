# FOLO Studio design direction

Written after round 4 by a design-director pass (Claude Opus 5.5) over every round-4 screenshot and the source.
Round 5 onwards is implemented against this document; per-issue fixes come after the system changes in §5.

## 1. Diagnosis

The screens are competent everywhere and memorable nowhere. Each weak area traces back to the system:

| Area | Local / structural | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Data viz (5.5) | Structural | Four chart implementations (LineChart, Histogram, PrCurves, ConfusionMatrix) with different axis typography; every single-series chart is sky-blue with an area fill, so a run page is six equal-weight charts; hover opens six floating tooltips that repeat the legend values. |
| Typography (6) | Structural | 11 px in 79 places; hard-coded 9/10/15 px; mono leaks into numbers (Playground, PR/histogram/confusion ticks); panel titles 13/500 = body. |
| Colour (6) | Structural | Lime means primary button, running, hero metrics, "best" across incomparable datasets, selected cards, theme previews, sidebar overline. Status hues used as links ("See Health" red, "+1 queued" blue); queued grey on Overview, blue on Runs. |
| Consistency (6) | Structural | No shared table (eight hand-rolled `<table>`s), no chart panel, no page grid. Errors look different per screen. Mobile lists use three card patterns; the top bar shows a title on some screens, only the logo on others. |
| Spacing (6.5) | Mostly structural | Off-grid 10 px (16×), 5, 7, 3 px in components; no column system (8+4, 7+5, thirds), so panel bottoms and inner edges never align. |
| Responsiveness (6.5) | Conceptual | Phone = desktop stacked in one column. The run page on a phone opens with Stop then six charts. |

**The five structural caps**
1. No primitives for the three jobs that make up 80 % of the app: **table, chart panel, page/section grid.** Every screen re-solves them slightly differently.
2. **Colour is decoration, not meaning.** Hue should identify an entity (a run in Compare, a class); right now it is applied by default.
3. **Lime is diluted** (20+ uses), so it no longer reads as "now" or "act".
4. **Type scale too small and too wide**: 7+ sizes per screen, most text at 11–12 px, no panel-title step.
5. **Equal-weight dashboards**: Run, Model, Overview and Compare tile same-weight panels with no single focal object.

## 2. Principles

1. **Density.** List rows 36 px single-line; dense rows (per-class, detections, config, labels) 28 px; two-line rows only on phones (at least 56 px, rule 12). Panel header a fixed 40 px with 16 px side padding; panel body padding 16 (12 dense); gap between panels 16 everywhere. Every panel-like surface (ui/Panel, KPI strips, Health, cards, folds, callouts and their skeletons) draws its outer edge with one alias, `--line-panel` (`base.css`: `line.default` in light, `line.subtle` in dark), never a `--line-*` of its own or a theme override; a strip whose 1 px dividers are a line-coloured background clips it to the padding box (`background-clip: padding-box`), or the translucent edge composites over it and reads darker.
2. **Page grid.** Content max 1200 px (`size.content_max`, the content column without the page's side gutters: list pages, detail pages and forms alike), 12 columns, 16 px gutters. Allowed splits: 12, 8+4, 6+6, 4+4+4. Panels in a row stretch to equal height; panel titles share a baseline; plot left edges align to column edge + 16. A right rail is always 4 columns and sticky (top 64). **A small grid of cards never leaves one orphan:** cards side by side in a form (New run's dataset cards) take as many columns of their minimum width as fit, fewer when that would leave a single card alone on the last row (`ui/balancedGrid`, `use:balanced`: four datasets are 2 × 2, never 3 + 1; `balancedGrid.test.ts`), since a plain `auto-fill` grid cannot know how many cards it holds. The Datasets page's grid is a list, as Desktop's: as many columns of at least 300 px as fit (three at 1440 px with the sidebar open, four on a wider content column), a short last row keeping the columns' width, so four datasets are a row and a bit, never two screens of two huge cards; Overview's datasets are one row of up to four compact cards. **A panel at 768–1279 px uses its width** (round 28): content that would sit in one narrow column with half its panel empty takes the multi-column layout it has on a desktop (Dataset › Health's findings are 2 × 2 from 768 px, on the KPI strip's grid, an opened file list or warning evidence a full-width row of its own, one column on phones; Model's metric help keeps its two columns down to 768 px, each term over its definition on a tablet; Compare's side by side with two or three runs shares the panel's width). **A dialog or a frame is sized to its content and the viewport, never to a fixed or image-only height**: the image inspector from 768 to 1279 px is the viewport's height less 24 px above and below, its label list scrolling inside the rail with Classes and the image's facts pinned under it; the Playground's empty frame on a tablet is about 360 px, the prompt and the sample strip, and grows only once an image is loaded. The same goes for what ends a frame: a stretched panel's foot sits on its bottom edge (Overview's "3 models not ranked", never 70 px of nothing under it), a card's strip on the card's bottom edge so a row's strips line up whatever each card's meta line wraps to (New run's dataset cards), and a lone field takes the half column of the fields under it (New run's Start from beside Epochs and Device), never a 420 px control over two half-width ones.
3. **Typographic rhythm.** One job per size, at most six per screen: 20/600 page title · 13/500 panel title (`ui/Panel`'s 40 px header band, design/UNIFIED.md §2) · 13/400 body and table cells · 12/400 meta, table headers, legends · 11/400 chart ticks only · 24/600 KPI values, their unit and secondary part ("img/s", "/ 100", a duration's "m" and "s") 12/400 tertiary on the value's baseline and a pair's slash in tertiary between two full-size values, every tile through `ui/Stat`'s `Figure` (design/UNIFIED.md §2, KPI strip). At most one 32/600 hero number per page. No 44 px step, no uppercase overlines in app screens (micro only for key hints). Mono for identifiers, paths, logs, JSON and CLI, never for a number. Helper text 12 px, ≤ 60 characters per line. **Measure:** every helper or explanatory paragraph (Field and Switch hints, Settings notes, Health findings, a callout's Why? fold, an empty state's sentence, a dialog's note) caps its `max-inline-size` at `--measure` (`base.css`, or the `.measure` class), 40ch: a `ch` is Geist's 0, a tabular digit about 1.5 average characters wide, so 40ch is ~60 characters where a "60ch" ran ~90; no component sets a ch width of its own. **Inside a dialog the measure is the dialog's text column**: `ui/Dialog` sets `--measure` to `--measure-dialog` (60ch), so a note, a hint or a body paragraph runs to the 480 px dialog's padding (440 px) instead of stopping at 40ch with its right third empty. **Prose fills its frame on purpose:** explanatory text in a full-width panel or fold (Engine benchmarks' "How this was measured") is never one 40ch column at the far left of 1,200 px: the setup is a compact facts grid (term, value), and the paragraphs that qualify it sit in columns of at least 36ch (`repeat(auto-fill, minmax(36ch, 1fr))`), each still at the measure, two across where they fit. **Copy is for users:** nothing on screen names a developer document or a source path ("docs/BENCHMARKS.md's figures"); the reference figures are "FOLO's reference benchmarks (RTX 4080 SUPER)" (`referenceSource`), a figure measured on another GPU says so in words ("measured on another GPU (RTX 3060), so not used for this one"), and a wide estimate range says why on its basis line ("Wide range: not run on this GPU yet", `referenceBasis`). A space between inline content and a block (a conditional, a list) is written `{' '}`, since JSX drops whitespace that holds a line break ("served from(…)"; framework/PORTING.md §8). **Identifiers vs names** (design/UNIFIED.md §2): paths, file names (a `.folo` file a run started from), server ids (`r_…`, `m_…`), fingerprints, commands and logs are identifiers and always mono. An architecture (`folo-s`) is a name: sans, like a run's, model's or dataset's own name (its row, title, chip, crumb or link), the model a run started from (`folo-s-coco80`), class names and a device ("gpu:0", Run's Config, as Desktop shows it). Both go through one component, `ui/Ident` (a link with `href`), in tables, chips, meta lines, rails and sentences alike; it tells them apart by value (`identKind`: an architecture, or a model named for one, `folo…` / `yolo…`, is a name) or by its `kind` prop, so a call site never picks a font (`shell/shellLook.test.tsx`). Prose values in an identifier slot ("From scratch", "auto") stay sans, and so do named optimizers ("AdamW", "SGD"): they are names, not identifiers, and in Geist Mono at 13 px AdamW's struck W reads as "₩"; every screen names an optimizer through `optimizerLabel` (`run/resolved`: "Auto (AdamW for short runs, else SGD)", "SGD", "AdamW"), except a value cell, which holds one short value: Run's Config and Compare's table say "Auto" with what it picks as a line under it (`Setting.about`, §3 New run). Text that cannot hold markup (a native `<option>`, a palette hint string) never carries an identifier: a select names the entity only, the palette passes it as its own `{ ident }` part (the scan fails on an `arch` in a `label:` / `hint:` template). Commands are code: `folo serve` in a sentence is a mono span, a command to run is one `ui/CommandLine` (or `ui/Remedy`, which holds one), never a `<code>` cut with an ellipsis (the scan fails on a bare `folo <command>` in markup). **The checkpoint a run started from** is named one way everywhere (`ui/Lineage` over `lib/lineage`): the model's name in sans, linked, its server id in the tooltip ("Model m_shapes_n"); a file by its file name in mono, the path in the tooltip. **Group labels** are one style, `.group-label` in `base.css` (12/500 `text.secondary`, sentence case): Run's Config groups (Training, Optimizer, Augmentation), the loss view's Smoothing and Y scale, Settings' benchmark sections, the palette's groups; a component sets their spacing, never their type or ink. **Code in a sentence** (a server's message: "`folo train --amp` asks for them") is `ui/CodeText`: mono, each word of a span one unbreakable run, so `--amp` never splits at its hyphen on a phone. **Meta lines** use one separator, `ui/Meta`: one element per item, a tertiary " · " with 8 px each side, never a dot written by hand and never wide gaps (a callout whose cause is facts, "Completed in 53m 30s · best mAP50-95 0.604 at epoch 150 · saved as a model", passes them as `facts` and the title is the line's first item); a wrapped line never ends (or starts) on a "·", and `line` keeps a desktop table row's items on one line (on phones it wraps like any meta line). **Never ellipsize a fact**: an ellipsis may shorten a free-text name (a run's, a dataset's, a class's) only when the full name is reachable (its title, its own page), never a count, a unit, a status, an identifier or a distinguishing suffix (`shapes-hard-s` beside `shapes-hard-m`); every `ui/Meta` item is a fact, and so is any span marked `data-fact`. A row restructures instead: on phones it wraps whole items (a meta line never cuts its last item), moves a fact to a line of its own (Runs' failure cause over the epoch and dataset; Datasets' image total and class count over the splits; the palette's hint under its name), or drops the least useful part; Compare's phone legend is two columns of compact keys, each the run's whole name on its first line (wrapping at its hyphens, never cut) and its value with the status word on the second ("0.494 · failed"). The shoot's QA reports any truncated box that holds a fact (§6). **Tertiary is for skimming**: `text.tertiary` is for labels and metadata people skim (a stat's label, column heads, keys, dates, separators); figures people read are `text.secondary`: KPI captions ("best 0.604 · ep 150", "warmup 360 steps · linear", "at conf 0.39 (best F1)"), a rail's measured conditions, a benchmark row's sub-line, a ranked list's counts. **A note after a value** ("0.000833 (requested 0.01)", "AdamW (auto)", "360 steps (3 epochs)") is `ui/Note` wherever it appears (Run's Config, Compare's table): a real, breakable space, then the note in 12 px tertiary that never breaks inside (`ui/Note.test.tsx` fails on a hand-rolled note span). **Conditions are meta lines**: a value's conditions (Model's measured and reference latency) are a `ui/Meta` line each, so "RTX 4080 SUPER" never breaks inside, never a sentence in a cell with `overflow-wrap: anywhere`; an identifier in a chip (Compare's "From `fasteners-n-best`") wraps after a hyphen onto a second line rather than lose its end. **Wrapping**: prose wraps `text-wrap: pretty` (set once on `html`), so hints, help, dialogs and banners never end on an orphan; a key group never breaks (`ui/Kbd`), and punctuation after it rides along in `.nowrap` ("Ctrl V."). **Value rows** (KPI tiles, stats) are a fixed line box: a unit or exponent is `line-height: 1` and never deepens the line, so values with and without units share a baseline in every tier.
4. **Chart frame.** Header (40 px, `ui/Panel`'s band): title 13/500, unit or qualifier tertiary, controls right (at most one segmented group); in a narrow panel the qualifier keeps its essential words rather than end in an ellipsis (on a laptop the loss says its raw step only, its smoothed total being the legend's; the learning rate its power of ten, its schedule being Config's). Legend row (24 px) directly below, starting at the plot's left edge. **Every plot sits in an inset well** (`bg.inset` behind the plot area, `LineChart` `well`), so a plot reads as one object on every panel. **While a run trains its x axis is the planned run** (design/UNIFIED.md §3): the epochs still to come shaded behind a 1 px rule at the latest point (`LineChart` `future`), the ETA over that boundary on the first chart ("ETA 7m"); events are labels in the band over the plot in one caption style: "mosaic off" over its hatched span (`LineChart` `xMark`), and on a failed run's Validation mAP and Training loss "failed during epoch 24" at its last step (`xEvent`, Desktop's words: the cross says that it failed, the label in which epoch, the one the Epoch tile and the callout name), inside the plot's top where it would touch the mosaic label, as the ETA does; "So far / All N" in the first chart's header fits every chart of the page to the epochs trained, and an ended run's axis is what it ran. **Legend values are the readout**: last value at rest, cursor value on hover; one line wherever it fits the chart, else whole items in even rows (`ChartLegend` `legendColumns`: four loss terms are 2 + 2, never 3 + 1), and a narrow plot beside a wide one (Training loss in its two fifths) gives its "Latest · ep 38" note's room to the values (phones keep it); one shared vertical cursor across synced charts; no floating tooltip. Plot: horizontal gridlines only (3–5), baseline axis, y ticks right-aligned in a 32 px gutter, 11 px Geist ticks; the x-axis name goes into the header ("by epoch", "by progress, % of planned epochs", "precision by recall", "boxes by size, % of the image side"), never a caption under the x axis or over the y ticks; the one y caption is the zoom note below. **The exception is a chart of one quantity against another on the same 0–1 scale** (PR curves: precision against recall), where 0–1 ticks on both axes cannot say which is which: it names both axes at the axes, `xLabel="Recall"` under the x ticks and `yLabel="Precision"` over the y ticks (`model/PrCurves`), and the room for both is kept at every width, phones included (a phone's plot keeps its 190 px and the chart grows by the captions). **One tick rule at every width**: a fitted domain's step and decimals come from the domain alone (`lib/charts/domain`, `Fit.decimals`), never from the plot's height, so 0–0.86 reads 0.0 · 0.2 · … · 0.8 on a desktop; a plot too short for every tick at 32 px keeps every second one of the same step with the same decimals (0.0 · 0.4 · 0.8 on a phone), never a different step (`domain.test.ts`). A chart panel never contains a second legend, a paragraph, an overline, a floating "best" label (use the ink best ring and the legend's last entry, "○ best 0.599 @ 36", `legendBest`), or more than one area fill. **A y domain fits its data** (`lib/charts/domain`): a 0–1 metric runs from 0 to its maximum plus 5 % headroom (`unitTop`, so the data reaches at least 80 % of the plot), with 3–5 nice gridlines under that top, never a fixed 0–1 with two-thirds of the plot empty; under 0.25 the axis is zoomed and says so in its y caption ("Zoomed in · full scale 0–1", `UNIT_ZOOM_NOTE`), so a run stuck near 0 still reads as low. Every chart of mAP, precision or recall that is not 0–1 by nature (PR curves are) takes `unitDomain` (`domain.test.ts`). **A comparison shows the differences people came to see**: Compare's y axis defaults to "Fit". **The y domain hugs the data** (`unitFitDomain`): from the runs' 30th percentile (`FIT_BELOW`, never above a line's last value, so every end shows) to their maximum, each with 2 % of the span as room, then rounded outwards to a minor step (half the gridlines' step; the floor, which the qualifier writes out, never to a third decimal: `floorGrid`), so the top sits at most one minor step over the highest value and the floor at most one under the percentile: runs peaking at 0.61 read on 0.40–0.625 with gridlines 0.40 · 0.45 · … · 0.60, never 0.40–0.70 with a quarter of the plot empty (round 27); a top on a half step is the plot's edge, not a tick. The gridlines are the same nice steps and 3–5 ticks as `unitDomain`, the tightest domain winning, and they depend on the values alone, so one axis reads the same at every width; on a labelled chart the top also keeps the label row over the highest end, measured on the fitted span; a floor under 30 % of the top gives the axis from 0. **On a phone, by progress, the axis defaults to From 0**: its 210 px plot has no end labels, and a fitted floor cut the first ~18 % of every curve, the four lines entering from the bottom edge a few px apart with no cue, where a mark per entry would only pile up (round 27); Fit is one tap away in How to read this, and a choice made by hand holds at every width. The header's qualifier says where a fitted axis starts ("by epoch · y from 0.40", `fitFloorNote`; on a phone, the y caption, set right over the top tick label where the domain leaves a line above it, never a row higher with headroom between: `LineChart` `capInPlot`), as a zoomed one says its full scale, and "Axis: Fit / From 0" is beside the x-axis control (on a phone, in How to read this). A plot whose floor is above 0 clips its lines at the baseline. A comparison's plot is at least 360 px tall from 768 px up. **Direct labels** (`endLabels`) name each line where it is drawn: beside its end marker when that spot is clear (12 px from another label, `LABEL_GAP`, and 6 px off every other run's line, `CLEAR`: 2 px off another run's line reads as sitting on it), otherwise in the **gutter column** right of the plot, as is a line ending at the plot's right edge. **A line's label sits where the line ends**: a line that has ended inside the plot (failed, stopped) is labelled beside its own end marker like any other, never at the plot's right edge far from it (round 27: "shapes-hard-s · stopped", ending at 63 %, labelled at 100 %); only when no spot beside its end is clear does it go to the column, **keyed**: the line's swatch (its hue and dash, `KEY_W`) before its name, so it is matched by its pattern, not by following hue and height across other curves. The column is a list in the order of the lines' last heights, **each label in its own 16 px slot** (`GUTTER_SLOT`, the label's whole line, so two labels' boxes never overlap: at 14 px Loss's "box" and "dfl" read as touching, round 27) at its line's last height where it can be, a greedy stack centred on the ends that collide, so two lines ending together (Loss's box and dfl) are labelled one above the other right at their ends with no leaders (round 26: a "<" of leaders between them). A label in the plot that the column runs into joins it. **A leader never crosses another label or another line**: it is drawn only inside the gutter, from an edge line's end to its moved label; a line that ends inside the plot is read by its label's height in the column, never by a leader across the lines between (round 26 at 820: "shapes-hard-m · failed" set above "fasteners-s · live", its leader through that label and the blue line). Nothing floats mid-plot on a leader. A column holds as many labels as the plot has slots (`columnCapacity`): past that, the labels first in the chart's order keep their slots and the rest fold into one "+N more" entry at the column's foot, so no label ever leaves the plot (`endLabels.test.ts`). Every line end gets its label (from 560 px up); widths are measured, not estimated, and the gutter is as wide as the widest label (`endLabels.test.ts`). **One rule for direct labels at every chart:** a plot under 560 px (`END_LABELS_MIN`, every phone) draws none, short names such as Loss's "box", "cls", "dfl" included, and gives that width to the data; its legend carries each line's name, value and how it ended (`LineChart.test.tsx`). A chart with direct labels leaves them a row inside the plot: its 0–1 top is fitted to the highest end raised by a label row (`withLabelRow`, 24 px), so a label set above its line never sits on the top gridline. **One focal-chart vocabulary:** a run's focal chart (Run's Validation mAP, Overview's live panel) marks the best epoch with a hollow ink ring (`text.primary`, 1.5 px, the "○ best" entry of its legend, "○ best 0.599 @ 36"; pulsed once on a new best) and the line's end with the status end mark (`end`: a filled lime dot with the heartbeat while live, the running mark; a square stopped; a cross failed; a plain dot in the line's hue completed). Every run chart's first series takes it (mAP50-95, precision, total loss, the learning rate or the throughput) and every other series ends in a dot of its own hue (design/UNIFIED.md §3, Marks; `RunCharts.test.tsx`). A run's four charts share one cursor (`bind:cursor`): hovering or arrowing through one reads the same epoch in every legend. **Each mark has one shape for one meaning, whatever sits next to it** (`charts/endMarks`): the ring is always the best and always ink, on a live, completed, stopped or failed run; the filled lime dot is always the latest value of a live run; so best at epoch 36 and live at 37 read as a ring and a dot, never two identical marks ("oo", round 25). Only when the best is the line's end, or so close that the two would overlap (`MERGE_PX`, under 2 px clear: a phone's epochs 36 and 37), is the ring drawn around the end mark as one mark; the status mark is always drawn, never covered. **The glyph says the status; the hue says whose line it is**: on a line in ink a failed run's cross takes the danger ink, the status hue on its mark; on a line in a series hue (a run's own metrics, a run in Compare) the square and the cross take the line's own hue, never the danger red, which on a plot of hues reads as one more run (round 27; `endMarks.test.ts`); the live dot is lime on both, lime meaning now. **No series is hidden under the others** (`charts/layers`): a run that has ended (failed, stopped) is painted over the runs that ran on, patterned lines over solid ones within each, the shorter last. Every chart that draws end marks (Run, Overview's live card, Compare) goes through `LineChart`'s `endDots`, so the rule holds everywhere (`endMarks.test.ts`, `LineChart.test.tsx`). **The one brand moment on a chart:** when a run completes while its page is open, lime viewfinder corners close in on its best ring over 200 ms, hold and fade (`Marker.focus`, `charts/bestPulse` `CompleteFocus`; none under reduced motion).
5. **Colour by entity.** Hue identifies an entity: a run in Compare, a class in evaluation, a metric on one run's charts. A single run's metrics take the series hues (design/UNIFIED.md §3, `run/runModel`): mAP50-95 sky (`chart.1`) with the page's one area fill, mAP50 amber dashed (`chart.2`), precision magenta (`chart.3`), recall teal dashed (`chart.4`), the learning rate neutral (`text.secondary`). Compare keeps one hue per run. Loss: total primary; box / cls / dfl are three quantities, so they take the hues design/README.md gives them in both apps (box amber, cls violet dashed, dfl teal dotted). A per-step loss opens smoothed at EMA 0.98 (`DEFAULT_SMOOTHING`): the weight is per step, so 0.9 averaged ~10 steps, a fraction of an epoch, and still read as raw (round 27); the raw last step is stated beside it ("raw · smoothed"). Histograms and timing bars neutral ink. Area fill only on the page's single focal series, at 8 %. A series' **fills** (legend swatches, run chips, table keys) use `chart.fill`, the exact palette hue in both themes with the `chart.swatch_edge` hairline, as class swatches do; only its 1–2 px line takes the light theme's darker ink (`chart.series`), so a light amber run reads amber, never brown. **Series hue in text** (direct end labels, the Playground's JSON tokens, any 12–13 px text in a series' hue) is `chart.text` (`--chart-text-N`, `charts/ink` `seriesText`), which `design/contrast.mjs` holds to 4.5:1 on every surface in both themes: chart colours are for marks and swatches, never small text. **Patterned series** (dashed, dotted) draw in that darker text ink and, in light, at 2 px, so a dotted fourth run clears 3:1 in practice and not only per pixel.
6. **Lime means "now" or "the one action".** Allowed: one primary button per viewport, focus ring, running dot and live progress (a live line's end dot), selected detection, the completion focus on a best ring (§2.4). The best-epoch ring itself is ink. **Lime is never a control's state or a failed run:** a switch that is on is the ink track (`text.primary`) with an inverse knob (`text.inverse`), as checkboxes are ink (`ui/Switch`), so New run's Mixed precision never competes with Start training; the best ring is ink on every run (§2.4). **A status shows through its icon and its callout's tint, never its words:** a callout's title and its links ("Ran out of GPU memory", "Too short to learn:", "Why?", an error state's "Prediction failed") are ink, the icon takes the status hue, and a callout that asks for action (danger, warning) is a banner with its status's muted tint (`run/Callout`); this also keeps amber text, weak in light, off the screen. A field's own error message under it stays in the danger ink (§2.11). Never a selection (a selected row, option, chip or cell is the ink bar and `bg.active`, §2.7), metric text, meters, nav icons (the current section is `text.primary` on `bg.active` in the sidebar, the icon rail, the tab bar and the palette), selected cards (use `line.strong` + `bg.active`, neutral), illustrations, previews. Status colours: in a table the status's glyph in its hue and the word in ink (`text.secondary` for queued and stopped; `StatusBadge plain`), tinted fills only in banners; the tinted status pill (`ui/StatusBadge` without `plain`) is the title form, beside the name of the one run a screen is about (the run page's header, Overview's live card), never in a table, list or legend; **a selection adds a mark and takes no fact away**: a chosen card keeps everything it showed (New run's dataset cards keep their format; the check goes before the name); queued and stopped neutral; where a status has no room for its word (the collapsed sidebar's connection) it is a glyph with the word as its accessible name and tooltip ("Server offline"), never a lone coloured dot. Once a list's selection enables its action (Runs' Compare, at two runs), that action is the one primary and the page's create action steps back to secondary; until then (one run selected, Compare disabled with its reason) the create action stays primary, so a state never has no primary. Links are `text.primary` (underline on hover), never a status hue; a toast's action ("Open run") is a small secondary button in ink, never a lime link. **Status hue belongs to the mark, not the metric:** a KPI or stat value stays in ink and its status is an icon in the hue beside its words in the caption ("3 · ⚠ not trained on"; `ui/Stat` has no tone). The same for every value: a table's best value is ink at 600 with a tertiary "best" word (Compare's side by side, the Models list), never lime text or an accent chip; a value with a problem (New run's Summary showing a device the server cannot find) stays in ink with the danger icon before it, and the row's accessible name says "needs attention"; an inline action ("Lower the threshold") is a link in ink. **One condition, one severity:** a forecast over the GPU's memory is danger on its meter and on the line under it (icon + words), within 10 % of it warning, and the figure itself stays in ink. **Demo data is a mode, not a degraded state:** the sidebar's foot says "Demo data · v0.1.0" beside a neutral dot (a neutral flask glyph on the icon rail and in Settings › Connection), never the warning dot.
7. **Tables.** One system: the global `.table` class (`lib/design/base.css`) is the single source, so every table looks the same without local styles. ## 6. Checks in the shoot

`npm run shoot` runs three checks on every shot after its setup and lists what they find together, printed last, boxed,
and saved to `qa.txt` beside the shots (`qa-<filter>.txt` with `--only`). The list never fails the shoot; review it with
the screenshots. It must be empty before a round goes to review.

**`npm run qa`** (`shoot --qa`, run by CI's `web` workflow) runs the same checks without the shots and
exits 1 if they find anything, printing the list. It builds the production bundle (into `node_modules/.qa-dist`, not
`dist/`) and checks that, served as static files: it is what ships, and the fresh browser context each state gets
loads one script instead of the dev server's module graph. It covers where phone layouts break: every scene at the
`tablet` and `mobile` sizes it lists, in dark (or the one theme a scene is made for), and again in light wherever the
page shows a phone section picker (Run's and Model's sections, chosen by the scene or by default). Phone states run
under a coarse pointer (touch: 44 px targets, 16 px field text, the stricter case); a scene about the mouse or the
keyboard sets `touch: false`. Each state gets the scene's own
setup; the page clock is paused at the fixed time before the page loads, so a state never depends on how busy the
machine is (in the shoot it keeps running in real time). Several states run at once (`FOLO_QA_JOBS`, default up to 8):
about two minutes for some 360 states on our test machine; `FOLO_QA_SHARD=2/3` checks every third state from the second
(CI's three `qa` jobs). It uses the
installed Chrome, else Playwright's Chromium (`npx playwright-core install --only-shell chromium`;
`FOLO_SHOOT_BROWSER=chromium` forces it, `chrome` refuses the fallback). Desktop and laptop are left to the shoot.

**Blank sections.** A selected control whose content renders nothing is the bug round 19 shipped (the phone rule that
hid every `.rail` left Model's Details, Run's Classes and a queued run's Config blank behind a selected picker):
- every selected tab (`[role=tab][aria-selected=true]`) and every checked radio with `aria-controls`: the element it
  controls must exist, be visible (not `display: none`, itself or through an ancestor) and be taller than 40 px;
- every checked radio of a section picker without `aria-controls` (a Segmented labelled "Show", or one in
  `.phone-pick`): the content under the picker in `main` must reach 40 px, and start 12 px under the picker (sections
  share one geometry: a hidden section leaves no grid track or gap behind);
- `main`: the content under the page header (`.page > header`, or main's top when the header takes no room) must reach
  80 px.

Content is what a reader sees: text, images, canvases, SVG, form controls, and boxes with a fill or an edge (panel
frames, skeleton blocks); fixed bars (the tab bar, docks) don't count. A section picker's sections should therefore keep
their content in the page's own region, not in a column a breakpoint can hide. The check was proven against the round-19
rule: put back `.app :global(.rail) { display: none }` in `App.tsx` (in the phone media query) and `npm run qa`
fails on `run-live-phone-classes`, `run-short-mosaic`, `run-cloud-training` and `run-cloud-done` at mobile, in both
themes, and cannot set up seven more scenes.

**Cut facts.** Any box cut with an ellipsis (`text-overflow: ellipsis`, its content wider than it) that holds a fact: a
`ui/Meta` line or a span marked `data-fact` (§2.3); and any element in one whose content runs past its box without an
ellipsis (clipped or covered: round 25's "Completed in 53m 3" under the callout's button). A `clip-path` counts as
hiding only a box it hides (visually hidden text), never `ui/Meta`'s edge clip. A truncated free-text name alone is not listed. The check found the
round-22 cuts (Datasets' "6 clas…", Runs' "shapes-ha…", the live card's "bat…").

**Horizontal overflow.** The page wider than the viewport, and any element whose content is wider than its box
(`scrollWidth > clientWidth + 1`, innermost box, naming its widest child). It skips hidden content, ellipsis truncation, negative-margin hit areas, positioned decorations and deliberate scrollers
(`data-scrolls`, or an `overflow-x: auto` box with a fade mask). A plain scrollbar is not a deliberate scroller: a list
table that needs one at a width it was designed for is the bug the check exists to catch. All three share the list above.
